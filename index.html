The problem

One standard, three ways to gate it

ITU-R BS.1770 defines how to weight and sum the channels. What it counts depends on the gate, and the meter in your edit suite and the QC platform downstream don't always use the same one. On music-heavy, dialogue-light programmes the readings can sit well over a loudness unit apart.

Level-gated

Ignores blocks below −70 LUFS, then anything 10 LU under the programme average. The EBU R128 and BS.1770-2+ reading most meters show.

Most edit-suite meters, EBU R128

Dialogue-gated

Only counts the parts where someone is speaking. Music beds, montages and effects drop out, so a programme that's loud between scenes reads quieter.

Dialogue-anchored specs, some QC templates

Ungated

Averages the whole programme above silence. The original BS.1770-1 method, still found in older tools and presets.

BS.1770-1 presets, legacy tools

Correct to −23 on one gate, deliver to a platform that measures another, and the file comes back.

What it does

Built for the bounce-back

Reads your QC report

Drop the report that bounced your file. Gatecheck reads the platform's own numbers and works out the gain that passes every test, or tells you why gain alone can't. SDVI Qualify read exactly; any other PDF, XML, CSV or text report through a general reader you confirm.

Specs checked with their own gate

EBU R128 and R128 s1, ATSC A/85, Netflix, ARIB TR-B32 and OP-59 built in, plus your own, with short-term, momentary and loudness-range limits. Make a spec straight from a QC report.

Timecoded reports and markers

Every over, with timecode from the file. A PDF report for the client, and markers for Pro Tools and Media Composer so you land on the problem in the session.

Opens the master you deliver

WAV and BWF, MXF (OP1a, OP-Atom, AS-11, D-10), MOV and MP4 with PCM audio, and IMF packages. No WAV export first.

Correct to spec

A gain-corrected copy of a WAV in the same format, with BWF metadata intact. True peak is checked after the gain, so you know when a limiter is needed.

Command line for render farms

gatecheck-cli master.mxf --spec r128 --pdf report.pdf prints JSON and exits non-zero on a fail. gatecheck-cli report qc.pdf reads a QC report.

Accuracy

Tested against the reference signals

Every build runs the EBU Tech 3341 test cases on macOS, Windows and Linux before it ships. Level-gated readings, true peak and loudness range also match FFmpeg's EBU R128 meter on programme material.

The dialogue detector is Gatecheck's own. Commercial detectors differ, so a dialogue-gated reading can sit a few tenths of a unit from your QC platform's. The browser version learns that offset from your QC reports.

FAQ

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. The app, the browser version and the command-line tool are free to use, including commercially.

Does my audio get uploaded anywhere?

No. The desktop app works offline, and the browser version measures the file and reads QC reports inside your browser. Nothing is sent to a server.

Which files can it open?

WAV, BWF and RF64 (PCM or float, over 4 GB included); MXF, MOV and MP4 with PCM audio, with several audio tracks combined; and IMF packages (choose the package folder). AIFF and FLAC in the desktop app. Compressed audio such as AAC opens in the browser version for a quick look; measure the PCM master for delivery.

Is the dialogue gate the same as Dolby Dialogue Intelligence?

No. Gatecheck uses its own speech detector, so readings can differ slightly from a platform that uses Dolby's. Match a few QC reports in the browser version to learn the offset.

Does it replace my QC platform?

No. It tells you, before delivery, what your QC platform is likely to report and why, so fewer files come back.

Will it plug into my editor?

Today it works alongside any editor through the watch folder. A Premiere Pro panel and a Media Composer AudioSuite plugin are planned.
